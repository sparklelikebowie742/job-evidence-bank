# Seasonal weed management program across 42 reserves (3,000 ha)

- **Employer / dates:** Wingecarribee Shire Council, Dec 2024 – May 2026
- **NSW capabilities (rough level):** Plan and Prioritise (Adept); Deliver Results (Adept); Think and Solve Problems (Intermediate–Adept); Technology (Intermediate); Demonstrate Accountability (Intermediate); Work Collaboratively (Intermediate)
- **Skills / keywords:** bush regeneration, weed control, herbicide application, works program, seasonal scheduling, priority weeds, Biosecurity Act 2015, threatened ecological communities, Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016, Southern Highlands Shale Woodland, Robertson Rainforest, ArcGIS mapping, follow-up treatment, training new staff

## Situation
Wingecarribee Shire Council manages 42 bushland reserves totalling about 3,000 ha, including 80 ha of Southern Highlands Shale Woodland and 13 ha of Robertson Rainforest, both threatened ecological communities listed under the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016. Most sites started at 25–50% weed cover; a few were already below 25% and in good condition.

## Task
Manage bush regeneration across all 42 sites: about half independently, the rest with a team of two working as peers. Keep priority weeds under control and meet Biosecurity Act follow-up obligations.

## Action
- Built the yearly works program around weed biology and seasons:
  - deciduous weeds treated before autumn leaf drop
  - fast-growing deciduous vines, and vines that go dormant in winter, treated in summer
  - grasses treated in spring, before they seed in summer
  - vines still active in winter treated then
- Put the most heavily infested sites first, and scheduled sites with local priority weeds into the yearly program for strict follow-up.
- Set treatment method and visit frequency for each site based on vegetation community, weed density, site size and weed species. Re-sequenced works when weather intervened.
- Mapped priority weeds across all 42 sites in ArcGIS (species, location, treatment date, herbicide) to schedule follow-up under the Biosecurity Act 2015.
- Trained new team members on site.

## Result
- Treated about 3,000 ha across 42 reserves, including 93 ha of threatened ecological communities.
- Brought most sites from 25–50% weed cover down to below 25%.
- Sites already in good condition stayed that way, and remaining weed hotspots shrank.
- Built a mapped weed record across all 42 sites to drive follow-up treatment.
