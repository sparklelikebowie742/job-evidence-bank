## 1. Council has a large area of Cumberland Plain Woodland. Highlight your experience and knowledge working in this vegetation community, what the challenges were and how you managed them.

My Cumberland Plain Woodland experience comes from my Certificate III in Conservation and Ecosystem Management at Ryde TAFE NSW, where [CHECK: what you did and learnt, e.g. plant ID, weed control or planting, and where]. I haven't yet worked in it in a paid role, but I've managed bush regeneration in two other threatened ecological communities listed under the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016: 80 ha of Southern Highlands Shale Woodland and 13 ha of Robertson Rainforest at Wingecarribee Shire Council. The challenges there are common to remnant bushland, and this is how I managed them.

- Heavy weed cover across a large area. Most of the 42 reserves I managed (about 3,000 ha) started at 25–50% weed cover, with a team of two. I put the most infested sites first, scheduled strict follow-up for priority weeds, and mapped them in ArcGIS for follow-up under the Biosecurity Act 2015. Most sites dropped below 25% weed cover.
- Timing. I built the yearly works program around weed biology, for example treating grasses in spring before they seed and deciduous weeds before autumn. I re-sequenced works when the weather intervened.
- Protecting the native vegetation. I set the treatment method and visit frequency for each site based on the vegetation community, weed density and weed species.
- Getting plantings to survive. On a 3,600 sqm Robertson Rainforest planting, I ran fortnightly follow-up, regular watering and survival counts. Across 20,000+ plants that year, survival was 80%.

I'd bring the same approach to Liverpool's Cumberland Plain Woodland, and I'm keen to build my knowledge of its local species and weeds quickly, working alongside the Team Leader.

## 2. This position works with volunteers. Outline your experience with volunteers and providing customer service to the community.

I've supervised volunteers at two councils, and I've been a Bushcare volunteer myself for about three years, so I understand both sides.

At Wingecarribee Shire Council I supervised 10+ Bushcare working bees a month (120+ a year), each with up to 15 volunteers from a pool of up to 350. At every session I:

- gave a toolbox and safety talk, covered emergency procedures and the work plan, and set out work areas
- adjusted tasks and explanations for different ages, language backgrounds and fitness levels
- kept attendance records, gave first aid when needed, reported incidents and followed up with volunteers afterwards.

I also ran inductions, trained 15 volunteers in weed identification, herbicide methods and mapping, and supervised nursery volunteers while running the community nursery for six months.

Customer service to the community:

- At Northern Beaches Council I've resolved 100+ bushland service requests. I check land tenure in ArcGIS Online, investigate on site, organise contractors when needed and keep residents informed through to resolution.
- I'm restoring a 4,000 sqm eroded slope that residents had wanted fixed for years, and they regularly give me positive feedback.
- I co-delivered a National Tree Day planting for 90 people, running safety briefings, explaining the species we planted and promoting Bushcare. This was recognised through council's staff recognition program.
- I've run plantings with 3 schools and 10 community groups (up to 50 people each), and given plain-language weed advice at 4 Local Land Services events with up to 60 landholders each.

For me, good customer service means being clear about what council can do, and following through until it's done.
