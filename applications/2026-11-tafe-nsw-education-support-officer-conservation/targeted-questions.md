# Targeted question

**Please summarise your relevant experience in the Conservation and Ecosystem Management industry and the concepts you have learned in your experience that can be applied in an educational environment.** (max 300 words)

I hold a Certificate III in Conservation and Ecosystem Management (Ryde TAFE NSW) and First Class Honours in Wildlife Conservation and Management, and have nearly three years' experience in bush regeneration. At Wingecarribee Shire Council I managed regeneration across 42 reserves (about 3,000 ha), including threatened ecological communities, planted 20,000+ natives in one year at 80% survival, and ran the community nursery for six months. I now co-manage two restoration projects at Northern Beaches Council.

Much of my work involves helping people learn on site. I supervised 120+ Bushcare working bees a year, trained 15 volunteers and 2 new team members in weed identification, herbicide methods, mapping tools and scheduling, and supervised a work experience student. I've had positive feedback on three occasions for my tailored, approachable training style.

Concepts I would bring to supporting students:

- **Practice cements theory.** As a blended-mode TAFE student, I learnt best when theory was followed quickly by fieldwork. I would give students regular chances to practise the skills their teacher has covered.
- **Follow-up makes the difference.** In regeneration, follow-up decides whether a site recovers. With learners, regular check-ins by email, Teams, Moodle or in person keep them engaged and on track to finish their tasks.
- **Adjust to the learner.** I've adjusted explanations for volunteers, school students and landholders of different ages, language backgrounds and fitness levels, using demonstrations, visual guides and plain language.
- **Safety and good habits from day one.** Every Bushcare session started with a toolbox talk. I would model the WHS, chemical handling and record-keeping practices students will need on the job.
- **Accurate records.** Chemical records, BioNet entries and service request tracking have taught me to keep careful documentation, which would help teachers meet ASQA compliance requirements.
